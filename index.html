Mind-Evolving Belief Tracking for Closed-Loop Autonomous Driving

Yan Shi1, Kehua Chen1*, Jiarui Qi1, Yihong Tang2, Haicheng Liao3, Yinhai Wang1* 1University of Washington  · 2McGill University  · 3University of Michigan  · *Corresponding authors

Human drivers read intent. They guess what other road users are about to do and revise the guess as the scene unfolds, an ability known as theory of mind. DriveMind gives a multimodal LLM driver a mind that evolves in the same spirit, holding several hypotheses about the scene and reweighing them with every new frame.

Theory of mind

Driving means reading other minds

A slowing car may be yielding, preparing to turn, or reacting to an obstacle the ego vehicle cannot see. A pedestrian near the curb may cross, or may keep waiting. People handle this with theory of mind: they infer what others intend and revise that guess as behavior unfolds.

Most MLLM-based drivers produce one rationale per frame and act on it. In closed loop, that action shapes the next observation, so a wrong first guess can reinforce itself before the evidence arrives. DriveMind instead keeps several readings of the scene, including what other road users seem to be doing, and lets its mind evolve as each frame arrives.

Method

A mind that evolves with every frame

DriveMind’s belief is a small set of natural-language hypotheses, four in the main experiments. Each is one possible reading of the scene, with a weight for how well the evidence supports it. A vision-language model (Qwen3-VL-2B) revises the belief about twice per second in three steps, while a fast controller steers from the latest belief.

1 · Update

Revise each hypothesis

The VLM rewrites each hypothesis to fit the new camera frame and the action just taken.

2 · Weigh

Weigh it against the evidence

A second VLM pass scores each hypothesis from 1 to 10 for how well it fits the frame. Weight shifts toward the better-supported ones.

3 · Diversify

Keep other explanations alive

When the weight piles up on too few hypotheses, the set is redrawn by weight and near-duplicates are rewritten as different readings that still fit the frame, so alternatives stay in mind.

4 · Act

Steer from the latest belief

A diffusion controller plans the next eight waypoints at 10 Hz from a weighted blend of all hypotheses. It reuses the latest belief between updates, so control never waits for reasoning.

Limitations

Most failures come from belief updates that arrive too late or from too little visual evidence, as in very dense multi-agent traffic, fast objects that appear between two slow updates, or long occlusions. The scorer can also favor unsupported or outdated hypotheses and delay the correction. The number of hypotheses and the update rate are fixed for now. Adapting them to scene complexity, and adding other agents’ intentions to the hypothesis space, are next steps.
