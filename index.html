Mind-Evolving Belief Tracking for Closed-Loop Autonomous Driving

Multimodal LLM drivers usually reason from scratch at every frame and commit to a single reading of the scene. DriveMind keeps several readings alive as weighted hypotheses and lets each new frame reweigh them.

One reading per frame commits too early

Traffic is full of ambiguity. A slowing car may be yielding, preparing to turn, or reacting to an obstacle the ego vehicle cannot see. A pedestrian near the curb may cross, or may keep waiting.

Most MLLM-based drivers produce one rationale per frame and act on it. In closed loop, that action shapes the next observation, so a wrong first guess can reinforce itself before the evidence arrives. DriveMind carries a weighted set of hypotheses from frame to frame instead.

Method

A particle filter whose particles are sentences

The belief is a small set of natural-language hypotheses, four in the main experiments, each a possible reading of the scene with a weight. A vision-language model (Qwen3-VL-2B) updates it about twice per second in three moves borrowed from particle filtering, while a fast controller steers from the latest belief.

1 · Predict

Propagate each hypothesis

The VLM rewrites each hypothesis to fit the new camera frame and the action just taken.

2 · Correct

Reweigh against the evidence

A second VLM pass scores each hypothesis from 1 to 10 for how well it fits the frame. Weight shifts toward the better-supported ones.

3 · Resample

Keep the set diverse

When the weight piles up on too few hypotheses, the set is resampled. Near-duplicates are rewritten as different readings that still fit the frame, so alternatives stay available.

4 · Control

Steer from the latest belief

A diffusion controller plans the next eight waypoints at 10 Hz from a weighted blend of all hypotheses. It reuses the latest belief between updates, so control never waits for reasoning.

Closed-loop cases · Bench2Drive in CARLA

Watch the belief settle, frame by frame

Each case shows the camera frames and the four hypotheses with their weights, as reported in the paper. Each hypothesis proposes one maneuver: stop, decelerate, go straight or lane change. Type weight follows belief, so a hypothesis grows bolder as the evidence supports it and fades as it loses support.

      Limitations

      The failures we see come mostly from belief updates that arrive too late or from too little visual evidence.

      • Very dense interactions with many agents at once.
      • Sudden, fast objects. An agent that appears between two slow updates meets a controller still conditioned on the previous belief.
      • Long occlusions that leave too little evidence to correct the belief.
      • The scorer can be wrong. It may favour visually unsupported or outdated hypotheses and delay the correction.
      • Fixed K and update rate. Adapting both to scene complexity, and adding other agents’ intentions to the hypothesis space, are next steps.
